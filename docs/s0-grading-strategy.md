## Owner-review bias and proposed correction

During [#124](https://github.com/andikapradanaarif/ganesh/issues/124), the assistant presented a calibration answer alongside proposed completion/critical/unsupported labels and asked the owner to confirm. The owner confirmed one case, then objected that this was inefficient, biased and potentially wrong without measurement. The objection is accepted: stop those confirmation questions. Preserve the actual declaration as guided feedback and record exposure; agreement following disclosure of a proposed answer is not independent grader-accuracy evidence. Two further unanswered cases had also exposed proposed labels. A new display order cannot restore blindness for already exposed cases. The independence of older guided labels is not established here.

The following is a **prospective proposal**, not an adopted amendment or a new run allowance:

1. **Separate mechanical evidence from judgment.** Use the existing source/answer bindings, executable oracle checks where applicable, usage accounting and structural validators. They can establish identity, exact spans and objective outcomes; they cannot certify semantic relevance or turn an omitted criterion into a particular human judgment. Do not build another validator to stand in for that judgment.
2. **Collect judgments before showing comparisons.** A batch review contains the complete source, frozen rubric and full answer, with neutral IDs and a frozen order; it withholds assistant labels, saved grader labels, intended fixture class and arm/model identity where feasible. The reviewer records their own criterion decisions, evidence and uncertainty before seeing disagreements. Record any residual cues, prior exposure and shared authorship. Use the existing packet rather than a new application or case-by-case confirmation chat.
3. **Measure the right thing.** Separately report required-criterion completion, missed critical/unsupported flags, false alarms and unresolved cases against source-supported adjudication. Keep all cases in their frozen denominators, including missing responses. Report exposed/guided judgments separately. The existing 18 synthetic variants span six tasks, not 18 independent natural examples; their agreement is a development check, not general accuracy. No independent reference means accuracy remains unknown, rather than a convenient percentage against assistant labels.
4. **Make one finite grading decision.** Review the saved errors first. Distinguish an ambiguous rubric/reference from an actual grader error. Any justified correction is prospective and versioned, with its acceptance rule and total calibration ceiling frozen before new outputs; the historical failed gates remain failed. If fresh validation is needed, it needs unexposed, source-grounded cases reviewed independently of their generator, a named reviewer/model, exact disclosure and budget authority. Do not repeatedly tune on the old contrasts or silently switch models. If an acceptable arrangement cannot be established within the limit, semantic scoring remains incomplete.
5. **Keep the protocol's owner checks explicit.** The existing critical/unsupported/disagreement/unknown review and six primary-pair/three diagnostic-set samples remain required. Batch their review and preserve uncertainty. Automation or model agreement does not waive them. Removing those checks or substituting a human-only grading protocol would require an explicit prospective decision; neither is selected here.

This proposal reduces leading prompts and distinguishes measurable checks from unverified semantics; it does not promise that another model or blinded review will solve #60. No new grading calls, references, owner decisions or acceptance follow from documenting it.
